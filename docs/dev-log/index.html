4 November 2026 · Planted contact and a level side-trip path · Experiment 36

Let the blocked foot start the fall

The creator finds the sideways ukemi promising, but identifies an unnatural first step: the character rises vertically before rolling. The collision controller was raising the whole actor to clear a low hurdle, using the same kind of clearance path needed by a vault. That made the side fall read as a hop. The intended reaction starts with a leg that cannot continue through the object, followed by joint compression and loss of balance.

The side-trip controller now keeps its ground reference level. It captures the impacting foot at contact, clamps the footprint to the near side of the collision box, and holds that sole while the hip and knee yield. The contact phase lasts 0.50 seconds; its influence then releases gradually through 0.76 seconds. A controlled pelvis drop of up to 18 centimetres combines with torso tilt and twist, and the leg IK bends the existing joints while preserving bone lengths. Per-seed source entry timings skip the generated idle preparation so the contact phase can lead into an already falling pose instead of rising back to a fresh standing start.

Both low hurdles and tall solids now keep the sideways reaction on the approach side. The character rolls away into a checked clear route using the opposite-side pool. This replaces entry 35's low-hurdle crossover rule; there is no upward hurdle-clearance arc in the active side-trip path. Ctrl+A/D, the three seeds per side, per-entry random selection and synchronized comparison still apply. A blocked route does not launch a roll.

This round reuses the same six generated Kimodo performances and their existing processed clips. It adds a kinematic collision-contact layer and timing changes, rather than another generation batch or a force-driven fall simulation. The front/back ukemi sources and their approved behavior are retained. The library remains 98 motion types, 204 takes and 408 body clips, with the original 94-animation default packs intact. Record / Save take bakes the planted-foot correction, controller path and played skeletal poses; source downloads retain their original generated motion.

Local validation covers both robots and all six side takes: 20 contact checks and 26 side-roll checks pass. The first 0.4 seconds keep the controller root level, the blocked sole within 1.5 centimetres of its anchor and behind the obstacle face, and show knee flexion/leg compression without changing leg lengths. Compared bodies keep independent foot anchors. Two recorded GLBs export with zero validation errors or warnings and retain contact/seed events. Staged and live verification are recorded separately; these local measurements do not establish complete physical simulation.

Review the foot at the obstacle, the knee and hip yielding above it, the pelvis height through the first half-second, and the release into the roll. The foot constraint and level controller path do not establish learned balance, correct impact forces or complete mesh-to-environment physics. Visual acceptance of the transition and each body/seed remains open. Replay the left strafe collision and right strafe collision from the footer trials.

The automatic sprint pool consists of seed 411 and these four candidates. Generating more seeds provides alternatives; it does not establish that foot sliding has been solved.

Variety is enabled by default. Each new motion-state entry draws from a shuffled bag of eligible takes, without immediately repeating the last take when alternatives exist. The selected seed stays fixed while an action plays or a locomotion loop continues. Both compared robots use the same selected seed. Current-version Reject reviews exclude takes from automatic selection for the visible bodies. Three new forward rolls use seeds 1101, 2202 and 3303. The conversion removes the capture's reference bone frames before forming local rotations. It does not reverse the earlier backward clips. The exact prompt embedding was already cached, so this round did not load another Llama encoder or upload the video. Peak Torch allocation during the forward generation was about 1.13 GiB; that is not total GPU memory consumption.

Direction diagnostics distinguish the results: all three new native forward takes turn through approximately +360 degrees, whereas earlier seeds 101 and 202 turn approximately −360 degrees. Earlier seed 303 does not complete an inverted roll and is retained for manual inspection only. Those earlier files were initially named forward ukemi; the backward label follows the creator's visual feedback and the measured rotation direction. These checks identify rotation and inversion, not certified martial-arts technique.

Retargeted rolling poses need explicit target-mesh cleanup. The game derivative uses a common active source window across both bodies, removes horizontal root travel, and applies a smoothed whole-skin floor-clearance envelope sampled at 30 Hz. C08's armor needs substantially larger vertical corrections than B-REK43. This is an authored body-height adjustment, not a learned support or balance solve. The immutable generated motion and raw retargeted sources remain available separately, including their original floor-contact defects. Armor self-contact, weight transfer and recovery silhouettes still need visual acceptance.

A grounded standing character moving at least 0.65 m/s can trip on a solid object 12–70 cm above the local floor. Actual controller contact, travel direction relative to facing, a clear rollout and a contact cooldown are required. Forward travel selects a forward roll; backward travel selects a backward roll. Jumping, crouching, carrying, overhead obstacles and high walls do not enter this rule. A roll commits through its inversion; the final recovery becomes interruptible after 72 percent of the action. The controller supplies the obstacle-clearance trajectory. This is deterministic collision logic plus a selected generated pose sequence, not Kimodo inferring an obstacle response online.

While the game viewport has focus, Ctrl+S walks backward at 1.25 m/s, and Ctrl+A / Ctrl+D select walking strafes while keeping the body facing with the camera. Other browser shortcuts, text fields and modal panels keep their normal behavior; plain C remains crouch. A backward collision selects backward ukemi. A sideways collision against a grounded solid at least 12 cm high, including a tall wall, instead uses a side-specific stumble with one knee and a supporting hand on the floor, followed by recovery. That brace is a separate authored contact layer, not a full roll or newly learned fall simulation.

The expanded review library contains 96 motion types, 198 takes and 396 body clips. The original 94-animation default packs remain unchanged. Preferred and Kept exports can include the added ukemi motions; individual source exports preserve their raw motion semantics. Record / Save take bakes the controller and contact layers from the played chain and retains its variation events. Review quality per body, particularly sprint support phases, backward recoveries and the side brace, before choosing game assets.

Action trials provide reproducible starting points: forward trip, backward trip, left strafe stumble and right strafe stumble. Compare both robots, inspect the seed in the HUD and disable Variety when an exact preferred take is needed. Numeric controller checks and inspected frames remain distinct from the creator's next visual review.

3 November 2026 · Ledge contacts and high-drop landing

Grab the ledge, then keep the landing readable

The creator asked for a jump beside a high wall to catch its ledge and pull up, plus a superhero landing with one knee down, the opposite fist grounded and the other elbow raised. The earlier short mantle had no sustained hang. The new high-ledge trial uses a purple 2.65 m platform; the drop trial starts above a clear landing pad. The footer Action trials selector replays either setup on the chosen body.

The ledge sequence has load, leap, catch, hang, pull-up, top-out and settle phases over 2.45 seconds. Hand targets stay fixed at the lip during the catch/hold, then release during the pull-up. Calibration measures the original rigs' shoulder heights, arm reach and limb lengths after normalization to 1.9 m. The landing point must have standing clearance; hand-width checks reject corners without room for both grips. Existing low barriers retain the shorter vault/mantle behavior. Tagged walls at least 1.9 m high use the ledge sequence, up to a 2.85 m limit; this is not unlimited wall climbing. C releases the ledge, and an attack also releases it into gravity.

A real downward impact after at least a 1.6 m drop and 4.5 m/s downward speed selects the superhero landing. Small standing and high jumps retain their normal landing behavior. The authored pose plants the right knee and left fist, keeps the left foot supported and bends the right elbow upward. A short compression leads into the pose hold and a smooth standing recovery over 1.65 seconds. Fresh movement, C, attacks or Space can replace the recovery. Facing is held during the contact pose so orbiting the camera does not drag the planted fist across the floor.

These are authored pose/contact layers and controller trajectories over the existing generated mantle and hard-landing sources. No fresh Kimodo inference was run for this round, and the 94-type source catalog, two seeds, original meshes and default packs remain unchanged. Two-bone solving preserves segment lengths; knee-shell offsets are calibrated for these two robot meshes. The new runtime layers are included by Record / Save take. Original source and in-place pack exports retain their earlier semantics and exclude runtime contact/controller layers. The recorded samples below contain a continuous climb, walk off the platform, fall, hero landing and recovery.

Testing uncovered two useful controller defects. Floor support used a narrower footprint than horizontal collision, so an actor could begin falling while still overlapping an edge and become stuck against the wall. Both now use the same capsule-footprint radius. At action completion the old clip could also be sampled at an unrelated local clock for one frame while the next asynchronous clip loaded. Holding the last evaluated source time until replacement removes that discontinuity. Traversal source pelvis lift fades out at top-out instead of reappearing after the controller flight finishes. Comparison now follows actor facing, rather than sliding the second model through the wall when the camera orbits; it remains a pose mirror without independent collision.

On both bodies, checks cover reachable ledges, locked hand joint targets, top-out clearance and continuity, real platform drops, knee/fist surface clearance, raised elbow, ordinary-hop exclusion, recovery interruption, ledge release and recording/export. Two recorded GLBs pass structural validation with zero errors and warnings. This establishes the tested controller/contact recipe, not a physically simulated climb or acceptance of every silhouette. Hands, armor, self-collision and transitions still need visual review at different approach angles and speeds.

Evidence: contact/action checks · recipe, source hashes and recorded-take mapping · recorded C08 GLB · recorded B-REK43 GLB. Props and environment meshes are excluded from character recordings.

3 November 2026 · Input cancellation and contextual traversal

Separate game input timing from a generated performance

The creator reported that Alt evade could feel stuck, C played a static kneeling pose, Space could not interrupt either stance/action, and another mouse click could not restart a punch. These were controller failures. The old controller rejected new actions while busy and used the entire roughly six-second performance as its action lock; crouch waited for a full multi-second transition. More generated clips would not repair that behavior.

The new rule is latest explicit action wins. Evade uses a 0.32-second, collision-limited displacement burst. Fresh WASD movement, C, another action or Space cancels it immediately. LMB alternates jab/cross and every click restarts an action, including the click that captures the pointer. Space preempts punches. Attack sampling uses selected active windows at short authored durations, with a 65 ms pose blend. The original complete clips, source hashes, seeds, preview identities and review histories are retained. These windows are diagnostic candidates selected from source motion landmarks; they are not proof of correct combat technique or smooth acceleration.

Single C immediately selects crouch locomotion, with a held support pose while stationary. A quick C C within 300 ms holds the original crouch-down ending as a static kneeling/seiza pose. Movement leaves seiza into crouch. Space jumps immediately from crouch or seiza when there is standing clearance; a tunnel roof must still block it. A normal second C toggles out of crouch. The live game no longer needs to play the complete enter-crouch performance before accepting another input.

Browser modifier follow-up: Ctrl itself is not crouch, but the old key listener treated Ctrl+C as plain C while the browser also received Copy. Ctrl/Command browser shortcuts now bypass gameplay and clear held input; they cannot crouch, kneel, attack, move, pause or start recording. The explicit Ctrl+Space high-jump exception remains. Tests cover modifier key events, actual Ctrl+C presses, consecutive C C isolation and ordinary crouch-to-jump afterward.

Physical flight now has its own lifetime, separate from the animation action. Replacing an airborne animation cannot suspend gravity. Jump anticipation is 65 ms, followed by the existing source takeoff/air/landing mapping. Step-off ledges fall under gravity. Late asynchronous clip loads carry generation tokens, so an old attack cannot return after the user has already requested a jump. Critical clips are warmed in parallel; a cold visual asset can still arrive late, but its request no longer locks gameplay.

Space also tests the environment in the intended movement/facing direction. Close, explicitly tagged low barriers select a vault; reachable tagged walls with free standing room select a climb onto the top. Other cases use the normal speed-sensitive jump. Ctrl+Space forces a normal high jump. The blue training wall and gold barrier have a direct traversal-yard starting link; the HUD previews the selected Space action. Surface range, height, approach direction, destination occupancy and the swept route are checked. Roofs, benches, terminals and perimeter walls cannot become climb targets by proximity alone.

These traversals are authored root trajectories driven by generated skeletal poses, not learned collision avoidance or a physical climbing policy. The mantle approaches the ledge, briefly guides the hands toward its edge with a fixed-length two-bone solve, lifts, crosses and settles on top. Source pelvis lift is bounded to avoid adding height twice. Vaults lift, cross and land. A blocked route aborts into gravity. Exact hand/foot attachment, mesh-level clearance, multiple ledge heights and visual contact quality remain research tasks. Compare is an offset pose mirror; only the selected character interacts with the course.

Pickup/putdown attach/release events are independent of action completion: cancelling before pickup leaves the box on the ground, cancelling after attachment keeps it carried, and cancelling after release leaves it placed. Character recordings still exclude prop geometry and trajectories. The guided course now cues vaults nearer the barriers and completes in roughly 97 simulated seconds. This supersedes the earlier tour timing; it does not alter the original source performances.

Validation covers actual keyboard/mouse input, both C08 and B-REK43, repeated click/replacement, single/double C, crouch/seiza jumps, delayed-load cancellation, vault/mantle landing, ceiling and boundary rejection, committed prop events and finite recorded poses. Separate regressions cover all 376 clips, review persistence/export, controller presets, four viewport layouts and the full guided tour. Responsive input evidence · Authored source windows · Updated control study. Technical checks and inspected still poses do not establish final visual acceptance.

2–3 November 2026 · Controls and motion audit · Experiment 30

Study action expectations before adding more clips

The single-page control study compares RDR2, The Witcher 3, Elden Ring, Resident Evil 4 and GTA V on PC. RE4 means the 2023 remake; Witcher references the classic publisher keyboard chart. Publisher manuals, an actual in-game mapping capture, a platform input reference and explicitly identified first-hand reports are distinguished. These are documented references, not local tests of five installed games or a survey of players.

WASD and mouse input provide a common starting point. Space, E/F, crouch, evade and combat focus differ substantially. The published Elden Ring Japanese keyboard appendix conflicts with the familiar launch-layout report; both are linked. We cannot infer from that discrepancy that a patch changed every player's defaults. A successful game's action design is more transferable than copying its exact keyboard assignments.

The playable Hybrid preset separates exploration facing travel from RMB camera-heading focus/strafe. Shift sprints with a hold/toggle choice; Space jumps; Alt triggers the existing evade actions; C crouches; E shows and invokes a supported contextual interaction. R is reserved for reload, while Shift+R records. The unarmed lab has no reload system. Q/MMB recenters the camera and V changes the focused camera side. Original Lab controls remain selectable and the preset persists. Focus is not held guard; recenter is not target lock.

The inventory audit checks 62 referenced current motion types against the 94-type Game Kit and identifies five reusable earlier sources among the 90 Kimodo takes. Guard, footwork, block-counter, slip and the reviewer-labelled backward shoulder roll are useful inputs. Missing game-ready states are not necessarily missing source performances. Reuse and segment those sources before requesting more generation.

The thirteen audit families separate animation, controller and metadata work. P0 includes gait phases/pivots, sustained guard, real target-facing movement, short evade versus roll, fall/landing phases, responsive stance changes, carry contacts, action timing/context and input configuration. P1 covers weapon layers/reloads, cover/traversal and directional reactions/ground recovery. Swimming, vehicles and magic wait for a game design that needs them.

Code inspection matters: clip changes currently reset gait phase; non-jump actions decelerate travel, so a dodge pose alone does not implement evasive displacement. Busy actions reject input without explicit buffer/cancel windows. Source stance transitions can last seconds. Crouch-to-stand checks headroom, but every exit path needs consistent treatment. More isolated motions do not supply these missing connections. Each audit family has a concrete acceptance test and links to present candidates.

Evidence: nine sources and confidence limits · inventory audit and runtime source hash · local preset / input / persistence checks. Full rebinding, crouch/focus hold alternatives, camera input options and target lock remain work to do.

2 November 2026 · Failed prompts and pose-conditioned recovery · Experiment 29

Give the jump an airborne target the model must reach

Several detailed text-only jump requests yielded running steps or small hops. Shortening the language alone did not consistently fix them. Treating every result as a successful jump and adding an attractive game trajectory would hide the source failure. Those trials remain local before/after evidence; the selected public sources use the corrected round.

A stronger high-jump take supplies five full-body anchor poses: standing, loading, airborne, landing and recovery. We translate the anchors along per-behavior paths, then use Kimodo's native full-body constraints to predict intervening motion. Seven behaviors with two seeds produce fourteen replacement candidates. The existing high and walking jumps complete eighteen selected jump sources with a detected airborne interval. That detector is a diagnostic, not certification of a technically correct or balanced jump.

For the game, anticipation, flight and recovery are explicit windows. The in-place derivative removes the source airborne root arc so the controller does not add jump height twice. The browser maps those windows onto its game arc and chooses a jump from approach speed and direction. This is model-generated skeletal motion plus authored controller timing, not an end-to-end learned physical jump policy. Early/late landings, leaving ledges, support changes and land-to-run still need reusable states and careful review.

Evidence: running jump candidate · high-jump candidate · selected source/export checks. The promising avenue is combining a clear action request with meaningful geometric anchors, rather than relying on longer prose.

The first 30 Hz bake allowed fast armor penetration between keys. Baking at 120 Hz and checking skinned floor bounds improved export fidelity. Whole-avatar grounding during crossfades adds a guard; it does not establish planted contacts or seat/hand support.

We examined whether every motion should return to one default stance. Neutral recovery is useful in a random demonstration, so Random + idle uses explicit idle gaps. It is not a universal locomotion rule. Run-to-jump should retain momentum; walking transitions benefit from cycle phase; sitting ends seated and lying ends on the floor. Forcing standing idle into all of these would erase useful state.

Action-specific entry, sustained state and exit is the more useful organization. The random mode demonstrates sequencing. The basic WASD/Shift/jump controller is a conventional playback controller, with no MotionBricks inference claim.

Leaning into learned in-betweening

MB-Predictive focuses on MotionBricks’ distinguishing capability: four recent poses and one to four target poses condition a continuation. We can place an arrival posture at a different destination/heading and inspect the movement and timing the model supplies.

The first six tests used original G1 inputs: near/far arrivals, a new heading, crouched/reaching arrivals and a chain. A non-neural control interpolates the same endpoints over the model’s selected duration. It exposes learned stepping versus endpoint interpolation; it is not a quality benchmark against a fully authored controller, and the branches’ contact processing also matters.

Pose harvesting then sampled context, midpoint and endpoint from five Quaternius clips, six own capture recoveries and four originally conditioned model outputs. Human poses were fitted to bounded G1 coordinates with errors recorded. Source timestamps identify sampled postures; they do not impose the original exercise or timing on the prediction. Three combined sequences brought the public presets to 24.

A genuine chain uses the last four poses actually played as the next context. Discarded futures cannot silently become context. This matters once a collision controller edits the root or a recorded action sits between neural segments.

The promise is controlled approaches, departures and variations around meaningful poses. Sparse targets do not guarantee faithful ukemi reconstruction, correct support, obstacle awareness or physical balance.

Running arms: a diagnostic and an unapproved cleanup candidate

MeasuredWe sampled local bone rotations at 30/60 Hz, separating moving samples near neural segment joins from those inside segments. Rapid changes occur in both. For example, selected upper-arm/forearm within-segment acceleration percentiles exceed the corresponding join values. The perceived jitter cannot simply be attributed to stitching seams.

These are discrete quaternion-animation derivatives, affected by track interpolation, key spacing and sampling rate. They are not physical accelerometer data or a perceptual score. A Quaternius CC0 sprint has lower upper-arm but higher forearm derivatives under the same calculation; that does not support a global smoothness ranking across rigs/styles/speeds.

Other tools: compare the role and permission

Clip libraries, editing assistants, video recovery and generation can complement one another. Compare the job first: finished action, transition, contact cleanup, or reconstruction of a performance. Installation, compatibility, AI use and public distribution need their own checks.

Mixamo: a usable library with different terms

Adobe describes Mixamo as free with an Adobe ID, without a Creative Cloud subscription, and permits royalty-free game/film use. Auto-rigging targets bipeds. This is not an open-source or CC0 library. Official FAQ.

Formal additional terms restrict AI/ML uses including testing and improvement, beyond training. Our practical decision is to keep Mixamo out of pose conditioning and AI benchmarks without clarification or separate permission. Ordinary offline game-clip use is a different case. Raw-asset redistribution restrictions also mean we should not place it in this public downloadable library as if it were CC0. Formal additional terms; Adobe distribution FAQ.

Cascadeur: an offline editing experiment

Cascadeur is proprietary. Free is non-commercial and exports CASC projects only. An export-capable plan is needed for our game interchange roundtrip: the current Indie grid lists FBX/DAE/USD/GLTF with an eligibility threshold, while Pro adds features including environment interaction and retargeting. Plans, trial scope and regional prices need checking before a decision. Vendor plan comparison.

AutoPhysics is an editing assistant with a configured centre of mass and geometry; it can report an impossible solution requiring manual pose/timing changes. It does not guarantee that our crouch balances. A copied EVA rig and permitted clips would make a controlled editing trial. Vendor Blender guidance favors binary FBX and warns against Automatic Bone Orientation changing joint rotations. No Cascadeur installation, purchase, exported comparison or balance fix has been completed. AutoPhysics documentation; Blender roundtrip guidance.

Kimodo: a separate semantic clip source

Official Kimodo supports text and motion constraints on model-specific skeletons. It could supply short action ideas for comparison with the current transition workflow. SOMA/G1 model choices, retargeting and output conventions require independent validation. A future six-clip trial would start with official SOMA-RP-v1.2, three short actions and two seeds each, with an optional matched G1 comparison. It has not run. Code, model and text-encoder terms remain separate. Official repository.

Vendor guidance estimates about 17 GB VRAM for fully GPU generation; moving the text encoder to CPU reduces GPU demand below 3 GB with slower encoding. These are vendor figures, not local measurements. The official path needs its own environment and gated text-encoder access; no account permissions or downloads were assumed. The existing 24 GB 4090 appears suitable for a controlled trial, subject to setup and cache inventory. Quick start; Installation.

Limits include at most ten seconds per prompt, fewer than twenty constrained frames per constraint type, foot skating or inexact constraints, and presently weak G1 postprocessing. Generated poses alone do not supply dynamic character control. Official limitations.

The community Blender bridge is another layer

The Kimodo Blender Bridge is a community integration, not NVIDIA’s official Blender add-on. Its manifest declares v1.5.8, Blender ≥4.2 and GPL-2.0-or-later; its referenced LICENSE file returned 404 during review. It launches external Python, defaults to an unofficial offline fork/older SOMA model and can create environments/caches in the home drive. A future test should select a separate G:-drive environment/caches and preserve existing Blender/MotionBricks installations. Meeting a manifest minimum is not a completed local compatibility test.
